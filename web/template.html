Follow the money until it comes back.

Accounts are nodes and transfers are directed edges. LoopTrace finds money that travels in a circle back to its source, the layering step of laundering, using Tarjan's SCC algorithm and a time-pruned DFS written in C, then ranks every loop by risk.

Build the graph

Each transfer is pushed onto the front of its sender's adjacency list in O(1). The whole graph lives in one struct with fixed arrays and no malloc, so the same C file runs in a terminal and in this page.

Split into SCCs with Tarjan

Money can only return to an account through its strongly connected component. One O(V + E) pass clears every account that sits alone in its SCC.

Enumerate loops with a pruned DFS

Inside each SCC, a depth-limited DFS starts from the smallest account id and only visits larger ids, so each loop is found once. A branch is cut when its hops go back in time twice or spread wider than the time window.

Score and rank

Every loop gets a 0–100 score from how much of the money survives, how quickly the hops follow each other, the loop's shape and its size. A heap sort puts the riskiest loop first.
